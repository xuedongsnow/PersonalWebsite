Bajillions Early Math

A research-driven platform offering 100+ early math games, hands-on activities, assessment, and classroom analytics.

Client
Marsico Institute for Early Learning, University of Denver
Product
  • Mobile app
  • HTML games
What is Bajillions? Bajillions trailer

Bajillions is on a mission to make high quality math content universally accessible. It consists of a mobile app with 100+ math games, recommendation system, assessment analytics, and classroom management tools.

Bajillions was funded by IES and the Gates Foundation, created by a 10+ team at FableVision Studios in collaboration with researchers at the Marsico Institute, University of Denver.

Who’s it for?
Children 3–8 years old
Credit
Lead Producer, 2022–2026, @ FableVision Studios

Personally, I think the most remarkable aspects of Bajillions are its robust content library and recommendation system.

Within each game, there are several layers of content and instructional feedback that can tailor to the child’s gameplay. At a higher level, the app suggests the next game that best matches the child’s math level based on their past gameplay.

Beyond games, we built a classroom management platform that connects teachers and caretakers to a vast collection of hands-on math activities. This way, the adults can plan in-person lessons that are supplemented and informed by Bajillions games.

Creating Bajillions was deeply rewarding, but also challenging. The teams collaborated to overcome the research-to-product gap. Discussions sometimes end with more questions than answers. Funding and time constraints were constantly at play. I personally grew tremendously, and felt my product management craft evolving constantly to meet the demand of the moment.

Project AMI: Open-Source Games for Museums

A collection of playable experiences designed for museums in Europe and the U.S. that runs the spectrum of location-based games, physical games, and digital web games designed for tablets as well as mobile devices.

Client
Max Kohler Foundation
Product
  • Web games
  • Print materials
Visit Project AMI

“AMI” stands for “Art + Museums Interacting”. Project AMI envisions museums as interactive, open, and meaningful spaces for all.

Project AMI brings together a community of Swiss and U.S. museum educators. Structured as monthly game-design sprints, the cohort designed 20 original interactive and game-based prototypes for art and history education.

The final prototypes are open-source, so any museum educator is welcome to make changes, customize the content, and bring innovative programming to their community.

Who’s it for?
Museum visitors of all ages
Partners
  • SFMoMA
  • Swiss National Museum
  • Kunstmuseum Basel
Credit
Producer, 2024–2025, @ FableVision Studios

As a museum-lover in my personal life, I had so much fun producing Project AMI.

Between January and August 2024, I got to work with 7 museum partners (~1 per month).

Each design cycle included 4–5 meetings and always ended with an in-person playtest event using the game prototype that we built that month.

PAW Patrol Podcast

The iconic canine champions are now on an audio adventure! 10 15-min episodes invite kids to ride along various rescue missions taking place in Adventure Bay.

Client
Nickelodeon
Product
Ten 15-minute podcast episodes
Watch the trailer Listen to the podcast

The Paw Patrol universe needs no introduction. What makes the podcast format special is that the characters break the fourth wall.

In every episode, kids are directly encouraged to move their hands, use their voice, and observe their surroundings. Listening to the show on a car ride, kids can turn everyday sightings and ordinary objects into exciting plot devices that help the pups save the day.

Who’s it for?
2–6-year-olds
Credit
Co-Producer, 2023–2025, @ Audiyo-yo, FableVision Studios

LOC Mixtape: An Audiyo-yo Original Podcast

An original podcast series that highlights audio primary sources from the Library of Congress archive. Each episode presents a unique historical recording interpreted by a historian and a contemporary musician who creates a “remix”.

Client
The Library of Congress, Lewis-Houghton Initiative
Product
  • Eight 30-min podcast episodes
  • Curriculum program and classroom materials
Listen to the podcast

Hosted by Postell Pringle, LOC Mixtape digs into the stacks of the largest library in the world, The Library of Congress, to make the ultimate mixtape.

Each episode connects historians and performance artists to discuss who made these historical recordings and why. A free curriculum program is developed in tandem to empower high school students to engage with the primary sources and to make their own original “remix”.

Who’s it for?
9th–12th grade students
Credit
Producer, 2024–2026

The Mixtape podcast takes a totally original approach to music and history education using primary sources: to combine the old and the new, to connect the universal and the personal. With the creative leadership of Anne Richards, Executive Producer of the podcast and VP of the Audiyo-yo podcast department, Mixtape offered me a chance to experience building something from 0 to 1 with the help of great people.

In this sense, Mixtape is the perfect recipe of what I find educational media production to be energizing — that one can present extant knowledge in a creative format, engaging a single viewer through a larger narrative, while having fun working with really great people.

Civics! An American Musical

An online game for middle school students that teaches how to analyze primary sources and promotes the importance of civics.

Client
The Library of Congress
Product
  • Web game
  • Curriculum and classroom materials
Watch the trailer Play the game

In this game, the player takes on the role of a Broadway musical producer who adapts true events from United States history for the stage.

There are 5 topics ranging from Desegregation to Title IX. The player must analyze curated primary sources from the Library of Congress in various mini-games, making creative choices such as costume, lyrics, and props that come together to shape the final musical.

Who’s it for?
  • 6th–8th grade students
  • Teachers of history, social science, and music
Credit
Producer, 2022–2024

This project was a precious nugget of all the things that I love producing: original art and script, original music, animated explainer videos — all rooted in historical content and a robust learning framework.

Between 2022 and 2023, I led the team to create a new mini-game nested in a new content module themed after the ADA. Then, to assess how the new mini-game is received by students, I organized a 40+ student survey with positive findings. This led to the decision of incorporating the new mini-game into 4 existing modules. In 2024, I supervised the development of another historical content module themed around Title IX.

Most projects I produce start with a blank canvas, but the Civics game was already launched when I took it on. My focus was on its expansion, so I took away a different but valuable perspective.

I learned to balance between following conventions vs. challenging them, adapting to constraints vs. improving the system.

Digital Tool for Assessing Kindergarten Readiness

A mobile app developed to support research on early self-regulation and executive function assessment.

Client
Oregon State University, funded by the Gates Foundation
Product
Mobile app
What is HTKS? 2026 Publication

The Head-Toes-Knees-Shoulders (HTKS) Measure is a decade-long research project on early childhood self-regulation. In preschool and early elementary school, performance on HTKS predicts early literacy, math and vocabulary.

The mobile app that we developed, called “HTKS-kids”, adapts the latest research-based assessment into a child-friendly mobile experience that takes ~10 minutes to administer. At the end of each assessment, teachers can see a simple report of the child’s skills, along with recommended activities to support the child’s development.

Who’s it for?
4–8 year-olds
Credit
Producer, 2025–2026

It’s a distinct experience to be embedded in a research project that comes with years of insights and iterations. There, production management is first learning, and then, creative translation.

On the HTKS project, I took on the challenge of learning quickly and precisely about the HTKS research. This enabled me to map out the flow of the app based on the real-life research protocol, guide the organization of its contents, and help define the scoring logic.

While my days as a social science undergrad are many chapters ago, I’m always ready to get up close to academic concepts and research findings. Reflecting on the HTKS project, it was an experience where I’m happy for my team to lean on my experience managing academic research projects, and my ability to ingest new information.
